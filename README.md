## 🦊 The honest pitch

Kitsumi started from one problem: *"I want to study Japanese from anime - without spending 40 minutes gluing five plugins together before episode 7."*

So it collapses the whole immersion workflow into the page you're already on: gets subtitles, puts them over the video, then you hover a word, understand it, and keep going.

**What it does:** deletes the setup tax. Searching subs, syncing them, finding dictionaries, cutting audio for a card - that's overhead, and overhead is worth automating.

**What it does not do:** learn the language for you. Hovering a word is *recognition*. Recognition feels like progress and mostly isn't. The part that builds memory is the part where you guess before you look, retell the scene, and produce the sentence yourself - Kitsumi hands you the tools for that, but you still have to do it. Anyone selling you passive fluency is selling you a fairy tale.

The deal is simple:

| Kitsumi handles | You handle |
| :--- | :--- |
| Subtitles, timing, dictionaries, lookups, cards, stats | Attention, guessing, recall, output |

It doesn't replace your course, Anki, textbooks or a tutor. It's the missing layer between *"I'm watching something"* and *"wait - I actually learned something."*

And yes, Japanese gets special treatment. Kanji declared war first. 🇯🇵⚔️

## ⚡ Your first episode, in about five minutes

1. **Install**, open a video, click the 🦊 icon.
2. **Pick your language** on the welcome screen - it sets up sane defaults.
3. **Download one dictionary** from the Dict tab. One is enough to start.
4. **Get subtitles** - *Extract* them from player, *Search* from providers inside Search tab, or drop in your own `.srt` / `.vtt` / `.ass` in local tab.
5. **Hover a word.** That's the whole loop.

Everything below is optional. Keep settings default until you need something.

## 📖 Hover a word. Know a word.

Subtitle text becomes an interactive dictionary: readings, definitions, part of speech, level and frequency wherever the data exists.

<p align="center"><a href="https://youtu.be/cirDXY3CkSk"><img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/189a8bc1-b207-40f1-bc77-ff08d0b962f8" width="900" alt="Interactive dictionary popup over a subtitle line"></a></p>

**🇯🇵 Japanese runs the full stack:** `Jitendex` · `JMdict` · `JMnedict` · `KANJIDIC` · JLPT levels · example sentences.

**🌐 Everything else runs on Yomitan-format dictionaries** - the same ecosystem Yomitan users already have. 46 languages ship with a ready-to-install catalog; the handful of long-tail languages with no Yomitan dictionary in existence yet are labeled as such in the list, so you're never left guessing which is which.

**📚 Bring your own:** `.tsv`, Yomitan `.json`, Yomitan `.zip`. Personal wordlists, anime terminology, domain glossaries - Kitsumi would like to meet your dictionary.

## 🧠 Study Mode and mining

Study Mode turns the current line into a working vocabulary list. Mark words you already know as **memories** - they turn gray and stop drawing your eye, so what's left highlighted is what's actually new.

Later, add **AnkiConnect** - the moment you actually want to make flashcards for the words you meet:

| 📝 Word | 📖 Reading | 💡 Definition | 💬 Sentence | 📸 Screenshot | 🔊 Audio | 🎬 Clip |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

Quick-mine hotkey, draft queue, configurable context. Cards are the *residue* of understanding something, not a substitute for it - mine the words you'd be annoyed to forget, not every word you saw. Your future self could spend hours reviewing every excess card you make now. Respect your time.

### 🎯 Comprehension target

Kitsumi can show how much of a line is new to you and underline unknown words, so you can steer toward material you understand **~90-95%** of - hard enough to learn from, easy enough to keep watching. That one setting will do more for you than any XP bar in this README.

## 🤖 Optional AI · 🖱️ Beyond the player

AI is **opt-in and dormant** until you paste your own key. No key, no AI, no quiet background requests.

| Service | What it does | Setup |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Groq | Cleaner definitions & example sentences | Your own API key |
| WaveSpeed | Generated scene imagery for cards | Your own API key |

**📚 Articles Reader** brings the same hover dictionary to ordinary webpages - news, Wikipedia, docs, blogs, or that Japanese site you should not have opened at 3 AM. **🖼️ OCR** (browser TextDetector or Tesseract.js) pulls text out of images and video frames. Both opt-in.

## 🕹️ Progression - and what it actually measures

There are **36 ranks**, from 🌱 Sprout to 🫀 Soul of the Language, 200+ achievements, streaks, per-language profiles, and a stats page that answers *"have I actually been doing this?"*

<p align="center"><a href="https://addons.mozilla.org/firefox/addon/Kitsumi/"><img src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/14338d4d-8b6c-4f39-96f6-cebff71b6b3e" width="900" alt="Progression, ranks and per-language statistics"></a></p>

> **Read this part honestly.** XP and ranks measure **activity, not ability**. Minutes watched and words hovered are a log of what you did, not a certificate of what you know. They're here because a visible streak is a decent reason to open the app on a bad day - that's the whole job. Level estimates (JLPT-style where a framework exists) are rough progress signals, not certifications. Kitsumi is not secretly your examiner.

No quests, no lives, no virtual fox whose emotional wellbeing depends on your Tuesday. It records what happened while you were busy enjoying something. Then one day you open the profile and go *"...huh. I'm actually pretty far."*

## 🔒 Privacy and permissions, stated plainly

| ❌ No accounts | ❌ No subscriptions | ❌ No telemetry | ❌ No analytics |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

Your study data, mined words and settings live in your browser's local storage. Nothing is sent to a Kitsumi server, because there is no Kitsumi server.

**Network requests happen only when you ask for them:** searching subtitles, downloading a dictionary, or an AI feature you switched on with your own key. "No collection" is not the same as "no network", and you should be suspicious of any extension that blurs the two.

**About permissions:** the extension asks for broad site access because subtitles have to be drawn over players it can't predict, including ones inside iframes. If that's more than you want to grant, restrict it - right-click the icon → **This can read and change site data** → *On specific sites*, and allow only the streaming sites you use. Everything above still works. That's a supported way to run it, not a workaround.
